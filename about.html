What This Is

Nobody knows.

Not about the big things. Not with certainty. The physicist doesn't know. The priest doesn't know. Neither do I.

That isn't an argument against believing something. Believe it completely. Most people do, and the ones who say they don't usually believe something just as firmly in the other direction.

It's an argument against the certainty. Against being so sure you're right that you'd spend a person over it — a brother, a parent, a friend of thirty years, someone who used to sit at your table. It's rarely the belief that costs you someone. It's the certainty wrapped around it.

And it isn't only religion. It's politics, and money, and how to raise the kids, and the thing that got said at the funeral. The particulars change. What tends not to is two people, both sure, neither of whom can really know — and something between them that quietly stops surviving the argument.

I don’t know what fixes this.
I know that being right about it hasn’t.

That's the whole of it. Love, and let live. Keep what you believe — hold the certainty loosely. And stop treating the person across the table as a problem to be solved.

There is nothing here to sign up for and nothing to join. Nobody is collecting your name or your address. There's a map if you want to see where the traditions and worldviews seem to touch, and a phone that nobody answers if there's something you've never been able to say out loud. That's all this is, for now.

Why It Exists

Someone had to go first

I was certain once, about something I could not possibly have known, and I said so out loud to someone I loved. I thought I was being kind. It cost me more than I understood at the time, and it took years to work out that the problem was never what I believed — it was how sure I was of it.

That's the entire reason this exists. Not as a lesson I'm qualified to teach. As the thing I'd want someone to have asked me, earlier, before I was so sure.

Where I Stand Today

The one thing held without qualification

No one knows the truth, and no one will, this side of death.

That sentence isn't on the front page, and that's deliberate — stated as a headline it becomes exactly the thing it warns against, a certainty announced. It isn't a fact anyone is being asked to accept. It's a position, held today, and by its own logic it could be wrong.

Which is the "let live." Not that beliefs don't matter — they matter enormously. Only that your conviction about ultimate truth isn't a hill worth losing a person on.

Two Words Worth Having

Ontology and epistemology

These two sound like gatekeeping and they aren't. They're just the names for the two halves of every argument anyone has ever had about anything that matters, and having the names makes the arguments much easier to follow.

Ontology

on-TOL-uh-jee

What exists? What is actually there?

The study of being. Not how you'd find out, not whether you could prove it — just what reality is made of. Whether there's a God, whether there's a soul, whether the world is one substance or two.

Epistemology

ep-ist-uh-MOL-uh-jee

How do we know? And how would we know we knew?

The study of knowledge — what counts as knowing a thing, what justifies a belief, where the limits sit. Two people can agree completely about what exists and still disagree violently about whether anyone is in a position to say so.

Almost every fight that ends a friendship is the second one wearing the first one's clothes. It looks like a disagreement about what's true. It's usually a disagreement about who's entitled to be sure.

These are here because someone special to me read an early draft of this site and said, kindly and correctly, that a site like this has no business using words like these without defining them — and that plenty of people would be shut out before they'd started. He spent a career writing and editing, and he sent the first two entries himself rather than just pointing at the problem. He'd want it noted that he offered them as suggestions I was free to ignore. I didn't.

If another word here closes a door rather than opens one, that's my fault and worth telling me about — hello@loveandletlive.com.
A Footnote

There was going to be a podcast

If you found this site earlier in the year, it announced one. Episodes were written, a feed was set up, and the whole place was built as a waiting room for a show.

There hasn't been time to record it the way it deserves, and a half-made version wasn't worth putting out. So it's paused — not cancelled, not coming soon, paused, with no date, because that's more honest than keeping a countdown running on something nobody can commit to.

The wanting to have the conversation never went anywhere. It's just starting where it actually lives: in person, locally, one at a time. Not by preaching, but by being.
